# Seasons and progression

Stand: 2026-07-22

## Why seasons exist

A season is not merely a database reset. It is Aquata's primary dramatic structure.

The baseline season length is six months. This cadence worked well historically: it leaves enough time for a meaningful rise, alliance politics, reversals, and genuinely large endgame battles while still guaranteeing a fresh competitive start.

The reset:

- restores scarcity and vulnerability,
- gives every player another credible race for power,
- prevents permanent dominance by long-established accounts,
- reshuffles alliances, treaties, and rivalries,
- creates a shared beginning and a meaningful finale,
- allows maps, rules, and strategic conditions to vary.

The desired contrast is strong: the first affordable ships and slow raids should feel materially different from the huge coordinated battles near the end.

## Seasonal arc

Every six-month season should have recognizable stages. The baseline pacing target is approximately two weeks of opening, six weeks of expansion, ten weeks of broad conflict, and eight weeks of endgame. These are dramatic and balance targets rather than a requirement that every capability switch on at a single global timestamp.

### Opening

Approximately weeks one and two.

- Minimal resources and production.
- Very few affordable ships.
- Long-feeling first journeys and consequential small decisions.
- A competitive race to train the command ship, acquire collectors, and unlock key technologies.
- Local targets and early relationships matter immediately.

### Expansion

Approximately weeks three through eight.

- Economic and technological paths begin to diverge.
- Players gain enough capacity to specialize.
- Scouting and map position reveal promising regions and opponents.
- Alliances become strategically meaningful rather than merely social labels.

### Conflict

Approximately weeks nine through eighteen.

- Fleets become valuable enough that losses change plans.
- Reinforcement range, diplomacy, intelligence, and coordinated operations dominate target choice.
- Multi-target pressure, defense, and deception become normal play.

### Endgame

Approximately the final eight weeks.

- Large fleets and command ships create major battles.
- Alliances pursue shared season objectives rather than only farming.
- The map and political situation move toward a visible conclusion.
- The season should culminate, not merely stop on an arbitrary date.

### Aftermath

- Results, notable operations, records, and stories are preserved.
- Competitive power is reset.
- The next season creates room for new alliances and strategic experiments.

## Official results and recognition

The regular season should formally recognize winners instead of leaving the meaning of first place entirely implicit.

Aquata uses an awards model rather than declaring one universal season winner. Each category crowns its own winner, comparable to an awards ceremony. The point champion is prestigious, but does not invalidate a player who deliberately pursued combat, economy, or achievements.

Before the destructive final battle begins, the game freezes the measurable standings. The five core award families are:

- point standing,
- combat points,
- economy,
- achievements,
- command-ship development.

The fixed structure contains eight season awards:

- five player awards for points, combat points, economy, achievements, and command-ship development,
- three alliance awards for points, combat points, and economy.

Achievements and command-ship development remain individual awards. If Aquata later introduces shared alliance achievements, they must be genuine group objectives with their own rules rather than the sum of members' personal achievement levels.

Every award uses a transparent definition published before the season. The awards are presented together after standings close and before the final battle. Winners receive permanent profile, alliance-history, and hall-of-fame recognition, but no competitive power in the next season.

### Historical ranking reference

The old game already exposed close equivalents to the four categories:

- Points represented broad current power. The score combined collector value, fleet replacement value, stored resources, salvage, command-ship experience, and ships under construction.
- Combat points valued destroyed ships, offset that value with own losses, and adjusted both through relative fleet value and combat bonuses. They were not a simple kill counter.
- Economy was represented directly by the total number of the three collector types.
- The achievement ranking compared total achievement levels and the number of fully completed achievements.
- Alliance rankings summed member points, collectors, and combat points.

These are strong product precedents, not formulas to copy without testing. Exact category names, component weights, collector equivalence, achievement catalog, tie handling for other categories, alliance-roster treatment, and the presentation of awards earned by an incognito persona remain open.

### Points award

Points measure a seasonal player's current total power and owned progress at the official standings lock. They are a broad net-worth and capability snapshot, not cumulative lifetime production or activity.

The total should include normalized value from:

- collectors and economic infrastructure,
- stored resources and salvage,
- ships and fleets wherever they are currently deployed,
- paid assets in construction,
- station expansion and other seasonal infrastructure,
- research and seasonal capability unlocks,
- command-ship progression and equipment.

Every unit of value must be counted exactly once. Spending resources on a queued ship transfers value from stored resources into construction; it must not duplicate or accidentally erase the same investment. Destroyed, stolen, consumed, forfeited, or neutralized assets stop contributing to the former owner's total when ownership or state changes authoritatively.

Combat points and achievement progress do not feed back into the point total merely because they are separate award categories. Previous-season prestige, cosmetics, community activity, and other persistent identity also grant no seasonal points.

The award always uses the authoritative total. Scan uncertainty, score-obscuring mechanics, delayed public information, or deception may change what opponents can infer, but may not manipulate the official standings calculation.

The alliance points award aggregates the authoritative point totals of eligible members under the final roster rules. Exact component weights and the public visibility of live point totals remain open and must be published before the season where appropriate.

### Economy award

The economy award is determined by the current collector stock at the official standings lock, not by cumulative resources produced during the season.

Collectors represent economic capacity that can still be built, stolen, lost, and defended. This keeps the economy race dynamic until the end instead of permanently rewarding whoever established the earliest production lead.

The player award compares collectors currently owned by each seasonal player. The alliance award aggregates the eligible collector stock of its members under the final roster rules. Collectors must have one unambiguous owner at the snapshot; unfinished construction and unresolved transfers cannot count for two positions.

If all collector types have equivalent economic value, the standing uses their simple total as in the old game. If the remake introduces materially different collector output or cost, an equally transparent normalized capacity measure must be published before the season.

### Achievement award

The achievement award uses seasonal achievement progress only. That progress and any gameplay rewards earned from it reset with competitive power; persistent profile and meta achievements are recognition, not input to the current season's ranking.

Players are compared lexicographically in this order:

1. Number of fully completed seasonal achievements.
2. Total number of achievement levels reached across the seasonal catalog.
3. Earliest time at which the player first reached their final standings-lock values.

Completed achievements therefore matter more than scattered partial progress. Total levels resolve players with the same number of completions, and the timestamp rewards the player who established the tied result first instead of using an arbitrary account identifier.

The standings should display all three values and preserve the achievement breakdown in the season record. Achievement definitions, tier counts, and any seasonal gameplay rewards must be published before the season.

### Command-ship award

The command-ship award recognizes the season's most highly developed command ship. The command ship is the player's tactical centerpiece and seasonal progression avatar, so its development deserves recognition separate from general points and combat points.

The published ranking must reflect meaningful command-ship development and remain auditable at the standings lock. Its exact comparison values remain open; raw experience alone is not automatically sufficient if it would reward repetitive arranged battles or low-risk farming over real tactical participation.

## Final battle

The regular season ends with a scheduled community battle after the official standings snapshot.

Historically, the community agreed on a final target and players chose whether to attack it or defend it. The remake should preserve that simple, inclusive choice. The battle provides a last shared story and a reason to commit fleets that would otherwise be protected until reset.

Its result is commemorated separately from the frozen season rankings. Participation may earn recognition, reports, or cosmetics, but losing ships in the finale cannot erase a player's official placement. The target, fiction, side balance, participation bands, and exact rewards may vary by season.

## Havoc and intermission

After the regular season and its final battle, Aquata enters a short Havoc mode for approximately one weekend.

Historically, Havoc compressed the global rhythm from one tick per hour to one tick per minute. It was intentionally unsustainable chaos: rapid production, fast research, constant fleets, and enormous material battles. That intensity works because it is optional, unranked, and strictly time-bounded.

Havoc should:

- preserve the playful material-battle feeling,
- run dramatically faster than the regular season,
- leave the official season snapshot unchanged,
- grant no competitive advantage in the next season,
- be clearly presented as a temporary sandbox rather than normal Aquata,
- end automatically after the announced weekend window.

The historical one-minute tick is the reference point, not yet a final technical timing decision. Whether Havoc starts from a copy of the final regular-season state or a purpose-built accelerated state remains open.

After Havoc, all competitive state is reset. A one-day intermission follows before the next season opens. Gameplay is unavailable during this cooldown, while persistent account and social spaces remain available so players can rest, review the season, choose identities, organize new alliances, and coordinate intended starting areas.

## The opening race

Historically, top players treated a fresh round as a speedrun. They optimized command-ship training, collector raids, research timing, and every early resource. Some woke during the night to act at the exact useful moment.

The race itself is valuable. It creates mastery, excitement, shared launch energy, and meaningful differences between strong openings.

Night activity must not be required for a satisfying season. Players who voluntarily invest additional attention, including at unusual hours, may earn a slight to moderate competitive edge. At the top of the rankings that edge is allowed to matter.

Aquata should preserve competitive optimization through:

- planning a sequence of orders,
- choosing mutually exclusive priorities,
- choosing and coordinating station positions around resource geography,
- reading the local map better,
- coordinating early targets,
- managing risk and reserves,
- using limited action or command capacity intelligently,
- adapting to other players.

It should limit compounding advantages gained mainly through:

- repeated trivial clicks at exact minutes,
- keeping the client open continuously,
- using automation to perform otherwise trivial inputs.

Candidate tools include queued training, scheduled orders, bounded planning windows, a voluntary lower-income safety posture, and clear completion notifications. Their exact form remains open.

## Progression principles

Progression within a season should:

- begin with a small, understandable possibility space,
- introduce new decisions rather than only larger numbers,
- unlock intelligence, mobility, deception, defense, and cooperation as well as firepower,
- make the command ship a source of identity and strategic direction,
- preserve hard tradeoffs between economy and immediate military capability,
- allow several viable routes through the season.

The opening must not become identical compulsory chores every season. Variation may come from:

- map and resource distribution,
- chosen starting position and local geography,
- neutral targets,
- seasonal modifiers,
- early specialization choices,
- altered strategic objectives,
- different alliance and victory conditions.

## Command ship as progression anchor

The command ship is both a combat unit and the player's primary seasonal progression avatar. Choosing its archetype is a defining strategic commitment: its type, skills, and modules shape the value and behavior of the player's fleets and therefore influence the whole style of play rather than adding a small universal bonus.

Its strongest tactical effects require the command ship to participate in the relevant operation. A fleet sent without it remains useful, but lacks the command profile that makes the player's main force distinctive. Because the command ship can accompany only selected commitments, its location creates a strategic choice about where the player's full strength and specialized capabilities are exposed.

Command-ship growth should come primarily from meaningful participation in combat and operations, tying advancement to commitment and risk instead of passive time alone. The exact archetypes, skill structure, modules, progression metric, reset details, and destruction consequences remain open.

## Reset contract

Players should know before a season starts:

- what resets,
- what persists,
- how the season can be won,
- when its main stages are expected,
- how inactive or late-starting players are handled,
- what records and rewards survive.

The published contract also includes the official standings lock, final-battle time, Havoc window, reset time, and one-day intermission.

Persistent rewards must provide identity and recognition without turning previous success into an insurmountable combat advantage.

## Catch-up and snowball control

Power growth and successful raids should create a lead, but the game needs friction against a permanent runaway state.

Important tools include:

- transparent attack boundaries,
- meaningful salvage and rebuilding,
- protection or recovery states after major losses,
- profitable targets appropriate to the player's current power,
- limits on repeatedly exploiting the same vulnerable player,
- strategic costs for overextension,
- season objectives that require coordination rather than only accumulated fleet size.

These tools should not erase earned advantages. They should preserve credible decisions and reasons to continue playing.

## Late entry

Players may join an ongoing season. A six-month cadence cannot require a new or returning player to wait months for a meaningful start.

The progression target is that an engaged late entrant can become strategically relevant around the middle of the field within roughly two to four weeks. They should be able to raid, make useful alliance contributions, and participate in appropriately scaled operations. They should not receive immediate parity with the leaders or invalidate the advantage earned through an excellent opening and months of strong play.

Catch-up should accelerate the player's path through obsolete early constraints rather than hand them a large transferable stockpile. Candidate tools include:

- personal acceleration of foundational station construction,
- shorter early research and training times,
- appropriate neutral targets and recovery opportunities,
- temporary protection from vastly stronger players,
- placement near players and targets of comparable effective power,
- bonuses that taper as the player approaches the intended season-age baseline.

Catch-up benefits must be bound to the late entrant's own progression where possible. They should not create a disposable fleet, resource package, or feeder account whose main value can immediately be transferred to an established player.

A player returning after inactivity deactivation uses the same zero-state entry path. They do not reclaim their abandoned station and should not receive both recovery value and late-entry value from the previous position.

Exact acceleration curves, protection boundaries, placement rules, and abuse constraints require simulation and playable tests. The intended outcome is current: accessible entry throughout the season, meaningful mid-field participation after two to four weeks, and preserved competitive value for long-term performance.
